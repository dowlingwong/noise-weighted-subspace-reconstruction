\begin{abstract}
Label-free scientific reconstruction learns representations from measured observations alone --- self-supervision in which the instrument-characterized noise covariance, not an augmentation policy, decides which residuals are informative. For Gaussian noise, inverse covariance is the likelihood-consistent reconstruction metric. Every ingredient is classical --- generalized least squares, the matched filter, weighted PCA, the linear-autoencoder/PCA correspondence, multilinear PCA --- and we introduce no new estimator; we organize them as a pair $(\Sigma^{-1},\mathcal{F})$, the likelihood fixing the residual geometry and the architecture the admissible representation class. Holding the metric fixed, we walk $\mathcal{F}$ up four rungs: optimal filtering, unrestricted rank-$k$ linear projection, Kronecker-separable two-factor projection, and factored attention. At the linear rungs the metric question has an exact geometric answer and reverses in practice: models sharing an architecture and differing only in the training metric rank oppositely under raw MSE and the likelihood residual, in a frozen ten-seed experiment and again in an independent twenty-seed replication, with a white-noise identity check at exactly zero. The covariance-weighted factors also recover the planted channel subspace almost exactly while temporal recovery stays weak. At the factored-attention rung, across two independent training seeds, the reversal does not appear, and the obvious explanation --- a near-degenerate whitening there --- is ruled out by measurement. The metric fixes the geometry; whether that choice bites is a property of the instrument and the representation.
\end{abstract}
