\begin{abstract}
Post-training monitors flag distribution change but rarely say whether the
evidence is most consistent with a sensor/acquisition shift, an unsupported
physical state, or a broken evaluation contract. We propose a controlled,
replay-based study of that distinction over \emph{declared intervention
families}---not unrestricted root-cause discovery, and not a maintained
software product. Declared interventions, named representation hooks,
deterministic contract gates, and calibrated abstention are evaluated on
NuBench point clouds and a fixed waveform reconstruction task, against input
tests, MMD, classifier two-sample tests, embedding distance, uncertainty, and
output-only monitoring. Because the waveform testbed generates its own noise
covariance, we also measure what each shift \emph{costs} the reconstruction in
the likelihood geometry of Papers~1--2, and test whether detectability and
consequence agree. Intended results: generic monitors detect N/S shifts;
layerwise evidence improves the hard N-versus-S separation; deterministic gates
isolate evaluation faults; and monitor response is only partially aligned with
real reconstruction loss.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope, continuity, and novelty}

Paper~1 identifies likelihood geometry under structured noise; Paper~2
separates residual geometry, architecture class, and training support. Paper~3
asks whether those contracts can be tested after training, and whether a
violation that is \emph{detectable} is also \emph{consequential}. Its artifact
is a reproducible experiment suite---configurations, adapters, hooks, replay
scripts, and reports---not a branded platform or a promise of production
maintenance.

The contribution is a controlled cross-modal design that combines (i) paired
sensor perturbations, support-composition shifts, and deliberate contract
faults; (ii) feature-matched attribution comparisons with guarded abstention;
(iii) a detectability-versus-consequence comparison on the arm where the true
covariance is known by construction; and (iv) a power--cost study of full
capture, sampling, reservoirs, and fixed-dimensional sketches. NuBench, the
waveform-noise generator, and MMD are inputs to that design, not claimed
inventions. Cross-geometry results test consistency, not causal
detector-design effects.

\textbf{Identifiability limit, from Paper~1.} Diagonalizing a covariance yields
decorrelated statistical modes, not labeled physical sources:
$\Sigma=\Sigma_1+\Sigma_2$ admits infinitely many PSD-cone splits and low-rank
factors are rotation-degenerate. Attribution here is over declared intervention
families under a stated simulator; a response profile does not identify the
cause of an unknown real shift.

\section{Failure contracts and claims}

We predeclare three labels. \textbf{N---acquisition/noise shift} includes PSD
or covariance change, jitter, drift, spectral lines, glitches, tails, hit
thinning, and channel/module loss. \textbf{S---training-support shift} changes
window composition toward physical strata that are sparse under the clean
training distribution; events themselves are not corrupted.
\textbf{E---evaluation-contract fault} includes wrong units, coordinate
ordering, output semantics, PSD convention, or metric implementation. E is not
a distribution shift and is not the likelihood-metric mismatch studied in
Papers~1--2.

\textbf{N versus S is the discriminative core.} E is decided by deterministic
schema and range invariants, so it is a gate result (E0), not a class in a
classification score. The informative separation is N from S: input-quality
evidence identifies N directly, whereas S leaves events uncorrupted and is
input-invisible by construction, so it must be detected from representation
position relative to the training support. If the layerwise signature cannot
separate N from S, C2 has no interesting content regardless of E.

\textbf{C1---Detect N/S shifts.} At a 1\% false-alert rate on nominal,
non-overlapping windows, the best monitoring signature detects at least 80\% of
held-out abrupt and gradual N/S shifts within five windows and is non-inferior
to the strongest eligible baseline by a five-point power margin. Delay is
primary in \emph{windows}; events and simulated seconds are reported per
testbed, and cross-modal statements use windows only.

\textbf{C2---Attribute with abstention.} The primary endpoint is macro-F1 over
$\{$N, S$\}$ on held-out cases, with N+S mixtures scored by multi-label F1 in
the same endpoint; E-gate sensitivity/specificity is reported separately under
E0. We predeclare \emph{superiority} of the full layerwise signature over the
all-generic-monitor arm as a paired difference whose 95\% CI excludes zero,
with a smallest relevant effect of $0.10$ macro-F1 on $\{$N, S$\}$, rather than
a fixed point margin: a real effect should not be scored as a failure because a
strong baseline performed well. Split-conformal distance in standardized
fingerprint space must identify an unseen family with AUROC $\geq0.80$ instead
of forcing a known label. This is intervention-family attribution, not
unrestricted physical root-cause discovery.

\textbf{C3---Trade power for cost.} Ten-percent event sampling plus fixed-size
sketches yields p95 replay-path latency overhead $<10\%$, at least $10\times$
less representation storage, and no more than five points of power/F1 loss
relative to full capture. Effects and intervals are repeated across modalities,
three NuBench model families, two machines, and CPU/GPU where supported.

\textbf{C4---Detection is not consequence.} On the waveform arm the true
$\Sigma$ is generated, so each N intervention has a computable cost in the
likelihood geometry of Papers~1--2: change in whitened residual, subspace angle
against the clean basis, amplitude bias, and calibration ratio. We predeclare
that monitor response and consequence are only partially aligned---rank
correlation below a threshold fixed at freeze, with both off-diagonal cells of
Table~\ref{tab:consequence} populated. This makes the likelihood geometry
load-bearing rather than cited: the consequence column cannot be computed
without knowing which residual geometry is correct. No analogous quantity
exists on NuBench, where consequence is limited to downstream angular error;
we state that asymmetry rather than implying symmetric coverage. All targets
above are frozen after development profiling and power analysis.

\section{Methods and testbeds}

Each event window records input-quality summaries, named-layer moments or
sketches, outputs, uncertainty, delayed task metrics, and calibration. Eligible
N/S baselines receive identical windows and false-alert budgets: corrected
univariate input tests, RBF-MMD~\cite{gretton2012}, a classifier two-sample
test~\cite{lopezpaz2017}, final-embedding mean/covariance distance, output
tests, and uncertainty/entropy. A regularized logistic classifier is fitted
with identical splits to five arms: input only; output/uncertainty; final
embedding only; all generic-monitor outputs; and the full layerwise signature.
The fourth arm is the strong baseline, committed to in advance. E uses schema/range invariants and clean-reference tests, not drift statistics.
Clean miscalibration is a model result, not automatically a software bug
\cite{guo2017}; latent displacement is normalized by clean within-class
dispersion.

\textbf{NuBench point clouds.} The first cell is Hexagon Ice LE $\times$
DynEdge $\times$ direction. The restored 1,358,099-parameter checkpoint exposes
a 128-D pre-head event representation. Module dropout, hit thinning, and time
jitter instantiate N. S windows use clean low-density strata in energy,
topology, vertex/containment, and multiplicity, estimated from training only.
ParticleNeT and GRIT repeat the study; another geometry is a consistency check
\cite{nubench}.

\textbf{Waveforms and the noise module.} The fixed task uses non-overlapping
4096-sample windows at 10~MS/s and sinusoid-amplitude reconstruction. Two
encoders are fitted on the chronological training split: the rank-2 noise-aware
EMPCA encoder of Paper~1 and the Kronecker-factored encoder of Paper~2, so the
waveform arm is not a single-model testbed. The module accepts or constructs
PSD/CSD models, samples seeded frequency-domain random coefficients with the
required real-signal symmetry, transforms them into time traces, and adds
stationary color, piecewise nonstationarity, lines, drift, glitches, and tails
to clean signals (Fig.~\ref{fig:testbeds}b--c). It validates units and
second-order spectral statistics. It is therefore a controlled N intervention
engine, not another dataset, reconstruction model, or physical-source
separator. S holds out declared frequency/amplitude ranges; E swaps coefficient
order or frequency units and changes PSD/score conventions.

Because the module generates the noise, both the assumed $\hat\Sigma$ used by
the encoder and the realized $\Sigma$ are known for every intervention, so each
waveform window carries a consequence record (C4) alongside its monitor record
--- one additional column, not a separate experiment.

Held-out severities, seeds, support strata, N+S mixtures, and one unseen family
prevent perturbation lookup. Windows and thresholds are frozen after
development. Intervals resample event groups and perturbation seeds separately.
Cost sweeps event rate, window size, hook count, embedding dimension, and model
count; repeat runs record machine, accelerator, timing method, and data/config
hashes. Null results require a passed positive control, adequate precision, and
a predeclared equivalence margin.

\section{Optional Track B: a collaborator pipeline}

The study above runs on public NuBench data and our own waveform task; no
external dependency is on the critical path. A third testbed on a
collaborator's pipeline would test what neither of ours can---whether a
standardized diagnostic conclusion \emph{replicates} outside the domains it was
developed in. It is offered as an option to accept, scope down, or decline.

\textbf{Scope if adopted, deliberately small.} Only the smallest supported
conclusion from E1/E2---one representation, one severity grid, paired pre/post
events---reported as replication or heterogeneity. Tracks are never pooled:
they share only the contract \emph{paired input $\to$ declared intervention and
severity $\to$ frozen representation $\to$ standardized response}. Raw
severities and latent distances are never compared across modalities.

\textbf{Feasibility turns on four answers.} (i) Input modality---dense
waveforms admit the noise module directly; hits or higher-level objects need a
different intervention channel and make the module precedent only.
(ii) Availability of raw inputs for paired re-inference. (iii) Which
intermediate representation extracts reproducibly. (iv) Which data are real
versus simulated, and what noise-state metadata exist.

\textbf{Three participation levels, to choose from.} \emph{(a) Review
only}---comment on perturbation plausibility and physical interpretation; no
data exchange. \emph{(b) Third testbed}---supply pipeline and representation
access for the reduced replication above. \emph{(c) Joint consequence
study}---co-develop C4 as a systematic-uncertainty analysis, where a surrogate
or fast forward model's approximation error is exactly a declared N
intervention and its measured consequence is what a systematics budget
requires. Level (c) is the closest fit to a physics-analysis interest and the
only one that changes the claim set.

\section{Smoke test and feasibility gate}

The selected 8.82~GiB NuBench release is checksummed; the DynEdge checkpoint
restores and the 128-D hook runs. Rescoring all 2,989,339 released predictions
reproduces published median angular errors within $0.055^\circ$:
$23.6548^\circ$ versus $23.60^\circ$ for CC/track and $56.1553^\circ$ versus
$56.12^\circ$ for NC/cascade. This misses a deliberately strict $0.01^\circ$
identity tolerance. On 256 development events, checkpoint and released
directions differ by $0.944^\circ$ median ($7.708^\circ$ p95); therefore the
clean gate remains open.

Both discrepancies are high in the same direction and of comparable size
($+0.055^\circ$, $+0.035^\circ$) on two independent populations. Rescoring
released predictions is deterministic, so this is more consistent with an
event-selection or metric-definition difference---a quality cut, a different
denominator, or median-of-medians versus pooled median---than with backend
numerics. We test selection variants before negotiating a tolerance, noting
that $0.01^\circ$ is $0.042\%$ of a $23.65^\circ$ median. A
module-dropout positive control is monotone ($15.28^\circ\!\to25.12^\circ$
median angular error; 10-NN retention $1.000\!\to0.393$ from 0--50\% dropout),
establishing perturbation, hook, and paired-metric feasibility without
presenting the pilot as a scientific claim.
