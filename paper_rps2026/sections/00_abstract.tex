\begin{abstract}
Label-free scientific reconstruction learns from measured observations alone: each observation supplies its own reconstruction supervision, while the instrument-characterized noise covariance determines which residuals are informative. For Gaussian noise, inverse covariance is the likelihood-consistent metric. Rather than proposing a new estimator, we organize classical methods as a pair $(\Sigma^{-1},\mathcal{F})$ of inverse covariance and representation class, separating measurement geometry from admissible model capacity, and follow this metric through optimal filtering, unrestricted linear projection, Kronecker-separable projection, and factored attention. The linear classes admit an exact geometric characterization. At the separable-projection rung, models sharing an architecture but differing only in the training metric reverse their ranking under raw MSE and the likelihood residual, in a frozen ten-seed experiment and in an independent twenty-seed replication. The reversal persists when the covariance is estimated from $2{,}048$ noise-only calibration events in one fixed sensitivity condition, while the covariance-weighted factors recover the planted channel subspace almost exactly but the temporal subspace only weakly. At the factored-attention rung the reversal is absent across two independent seeds, despite non-degenerate weighting. Noise covariance therefore defines the scientifically relevant geometry, while instrument and representation class can determine whether that geometry measurably changes what is learned.
\end{abstract}
