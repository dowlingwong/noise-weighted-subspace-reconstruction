## 1. Question and prediction

On a generator that differs from T20-S/T25 in (C, T), spectrum shape and the way nonseparability is built,
does the decided sign change of the product-minus-unrestricted clean-risk contrast bracket the first-order
point N\* = (c_U − c_F)/a − 1 that was computed and registered **before any fit**?

Supplement S4.3 (Proposition S4.1) gives, under centred Gaussian signal, independent Gaussian noise, a positive top gap, a positive-
definite chart Hessian H at the product population optimum and local convergence of the fitted product
branch: E D(N) = a + B/N + o(1/N), B = c_F − c_U + a, so the formal zero is N\* = −B/a.
The prediction tested is the sign pattern implied by this expansion: D < 0 well below N\*, D > 0 well above.

## 2. Generator G2 (fixed; `generator.py`, parameters in `protocol.json` → `generator`)

- **Format.** C = 6 channels, T = 64 samples, d = 384; k = 6 with (k_c, k_t) = (2, 3); column stacking
  vec(Z), index c + C t, so a product subspace is range(U_t ⊗ U_c) (T25 convention).
- **Oracle noise.** Σ = S_t ⊗ S_c, AR(1) Toeplitz factors with unit diagonal, ρ_c = 0.4 (6 × 6), ρ_t = 0.7
  (64 × 64), built by the T25 function `t25.science.covariance`; only the dimensions change. Whitening by
  the symmetric square root `t25.science.powers(Σ)`.
- **Signal spectrum (whitened, identical at every φ).** λ_i = 24/i for i = 1…6 → (24, 12, 8, 6, 4.8, 4);
  gap Δ_k = 2; tail λ_j = 2·7/j for j = 7…384 (the same 1/j law; λ_384 = 0.0365).
  tr K = 115.91, top-k energy 58.8 (T25: 206.60 and 57.0), tr K/d = 0.302 (T25: 0.807). SNR is defined
  in the oracle-whitened metric (unit white noise). The decay is power-law (1/i) instead of T25's flat
  top block and geometric tail.
- **Nonseparability by channel-dependent pulse-shape mixing (not the θ rotation).**
  - Common temporal pulse basis (k_t = 3): double-exponential pulses p_j(t) = e^{−(t−16)/τ_d} − e^{−(t−16)/τ_r}
    for t > 16, with (τ_r, τ_d) = (1, 6), (2.5, 15), (5, 35) samples.
  - Channel patterns (k_c = 2): q_1[c] = exp(−(c − 2.5)²/8), q_2[c] = (c − 2.5)/2.5.
  - Channel c sees every pulse delayed by φ·δ_c samples, δ_c = c − 2.5 (a channel-dependent timing skew;
    φ = samples of delay per channel step). Template (i, j): Z_ij[c, t] = q_i[c] p_j(t − φδ_c).
  - At φ = 0, Z_ij = q_i p_jᵀ and the template span is exactly a product subspace; for φ ≠ 0 it is not.
  - The top-k eigenvectors of K(φ) are the eigenvectors of K₀ = Σ^{−1/2} B Ω B^T Σ^{−1/2} (B the physical
    templates, Ω = diag((1, 0.7, 0.45) ⊗ (1, 0.6))), ordered by K₀ eigenvalue, and receive the registered λ_1…λ_6.
    Hence the physical top-k signal subspace is exactly the span of the delayed templates (checked to
    ≤ 1.6e−14: control 1.6e−14, A and B 3.3e−15).
  - The templates are the sampled pulses on t = 0…63, truncated at the window end: at t = 63 the slow pulse
    (5, 35) is still at 42% of its peak and the medium pulse (2.5, 15) at 7.5%. This is part of the definition
    of G2, not an approximation.
  - Tail eigenvectors: a fixed Gaussian matrix (key `y19-confirm-20261012/geometry`) projected off the top-k
    span and orthonormalised (the seeded-completion device of T20-S/T25). Column signs fixed.
- **Latents.** Gaussian along K's eigenbasis; records are drawn exactly as in `t25.science.make_data`:
  whitened clean signal z diag(√λ) Vᵀ, physical record (clean + white noise) · Σ^{1/2}.

## 4. How φ was chosen (population quantities only)

Rule (`predictions.py` → `PHI_SCAN`): on the grid φ ∈ {0.01, 0.02, …, 0.50}, with 8 restarts per point,
φ_A = smallest φ with N\*_pred ≤ 3000 and φ_B = largest φ with N\*_pred ≥ 1200; require N\*_A/N\*_B ≥ 2 and
the §3 checks at both. Reasons: both points in the brief's [300, 3000] window; N\*_B ≥ 1200 keeps both formal
zeros at or above N ≈ 1000, below which Supplement S4.3 says the local expansion is not justified (a threshold
inherited from the T20-S/T25 setting, see §11); the factor 2.42 > 2 separates the two predictions. The scan gave
φ_A = 0.12 (N\* 2917) and φ_B = 0.20 (N\* 1208); the full scan is in `predictions.json` → `phi_selection`, and
`predictions.py write/check` re-derives it. No fitted quantity entered the choice.
**Order of events (disclosure).** The rule's limits (3000, 1200, ratio 2) and the φ grid were set *after*
exploratory population-only scans of G2 (φ ∈ {0, 0.5, 1, 2, 3}, then 0.05–0.30 and 0.11–0.22; preparation
REPORT §4). At that time no G2 record had been sampled and no learner fitted, so no sampled or fitted quantity
was available to the choice; the B1 reviewer verified this.

## 5. Learners and scoring (fixed; the T25 oracle arm, `t25_production_20260930/science.py`, accepted closure `01f2f82f…`)

- **Records.** One pool of max(grid) records per (geometry, seed) from keyed streams
  `t25.rng("y19-confirm-20261012", seed, φ, "signal"|"noise")`; smaller N are prefixes (T25 convention).
- **Moments.** `t25.moments`: fitted sample mean, centred covariance, divisor N.
- **Unrestricted (EMPCA/CW-PCA population form).** `t25.unrestricted(W, S, 6)`, W = Σ⁻¹ (`np.linalg.inv(Σ)`
  as in T25 `fit_cell`).
- **Product (NFPA).** `t25.structured(W, S, t25.starts(S, 6, 64, 2, 3, ("y19-confirm-20261012", seed, φ, N)), 6000, 1e-8)`:
  HOSVD + 3 seeded random starts (4 restarts), projected-gradient ascent with Armijo backtracking,
  selection by the training objective; max_iter 6000 and tolerance 1e-8 exactly as T25 production.
- **Affine map.** A = `t25.map_for(B, W)`, intercept b = (I − A) ȳ.
- **Scoring.** `t25.risks(A, b, Σ^{1/2} K Σ^{1/2}, Σ, Σ⁻¹)["clean"]`: the exact expected clean risk on an
  independent record in the oracle Σ⁻¹ metric (signal + noise + offset). The evaluation is closed form, so
  the evaluation size is effectively infinite (no evaluation sampling error), as in T25.
- **Contrast.** D = clean risk(product) − clean risk(unrestricted), per seed and cell, both affine with
  the fitted mean, i.e. the D(N) of Proposition S4.1.
- **Optimization-limited flag (T25 rule).** A record is flagged if the selected restart is not stationary
  or the restart clean-risk spread ≥ |D|. A cell (geometry, N) is optimization-limited if at least one of
  its 24 records is flagged. Flagged records and cells are kept, never dropped.

## 6. Seeds, family and inference

- **Seeds.** 24 seeds s_i = int(SHA-256("y19-confirm-20261012/seed/i")[:8], little-endian) mod 10¹²,
  i = 0…23 (listed in `protocol.json`). The namespace string appears nowhere else in EXP; the seeds are
  disjoint from T20-S (900000–900999, 3000–3199, 4000–4399), T25 (2026092901xx, 900001–2), TR09
  (202610060300–419 and fixtures) and E-NL (2026100801xx and fixtures) (test `test_seed_namespace.py`).
- **Unit.** The seed-level mean of D over 24 seeds, per cell.
- **Interval.** Approximate simultaneous 95% Bonferroni Student-t intervals, df = 23, over the full registered
  family of **20** contrasts (5 for A, 5 for B, 10 for the control union grid): mean ± t_{1−0.05/40, 23} · sd/√24,
  t = 3.393. Decided negative: upper limit < 0; decided positive: lower limit > 0; otherwise undecided.
  A cell with seed SD exactly 0 is undecided. Same convention as the paper (T25: 864-contrast family).

## 7. Reading rule (frozen)

- **Geometry A, geometry B (each).** **MET** iff the contrast is decided negative at N\*/4 **and** N\*/2 **and**
  decided positive at 2N\* **and** 4N\*. The N\* cell is unconstrained. **NOT MET** otherwise; the observed
  pattern (−/?/+ at the five grid points) is reported.
- **Separable control (φ = 0).** **MET** iff no contrast is decided positive at any of its 10 N.
- **Overall (machine component).** `summary.json` → `overall` is **MET** iff both A and B are MET, otherwise
  NOT MET (as coded in `runner.reduce`; unchanged).
- **Overall reported reading (frozen interpretation of the machine outputs; runbook §4: "the separable control
  must show no positive contrast").**
  - **MET**: A MET, B MET and control MET.
  - **MET WITH FAILED CONTROL**: A MET and B MET but control NOT MET. A decided positive contrast at a = 0
    contradicts the first-order prediction (D < 0 at every N) and points to a pipeline or optimization defect,
    so no confirmation is claimed; the paper must state the failed control and report the reading under this
    label, never as MET.
  - **NOT MET**: A or B NOT MET (whatever the control), with both patterns and the control reading.
- **Optimization limits.** Reported with every reading; if a deciding cell (N\*/4, N\*/2, 2N\*, 4N\*) is
  optimization-limited, the reading is reported with that flag (it is not changed).
- Implemented in `runner.reduce`; unit-tested on fabricated numbers (`test_reading_rule_logic`).

## 8. Secondary descriptives (prespecified; no pass/fail)

1. At the N\* cell of A and B: the seed-mean contrast and its interval (first order predicts ≈ 0); and an OLS
   fit of the five seed-mean contrasts to D(N) = α + β/N with N\*_fit = −β/α, reported beside a_best and B.
2. Unrestricted excess Δ_U = R_K(P̂_U) − R_K(P\*_U) (the `risks` signal term minus Σ_{j>k} λ_j): OLS fit of
   N · mean Δ_U(N) = c + e/N over all 20 registered cells; c is compared with c_U = 3097.36.

## 9. Exclusions, deviations, what happens on failure

- **Exclusions: none.** Every registered record enters; optimization-limited records are flagged, not removed.
- **Deviations after freeze: none.** No grid, seed, geometry, learner, tolerance or reading change. A coding
  error found after the freeze is reported; a corrected run needs a new namespace and a new registration,
  and the original result stays reported.
- An interrupted run is resumed with the same command; existing records are never recomputed or overwritten
  (this is not a rerun). A completed run is never repeated.
- A NOT MET result is reported as a failed prospective prediction (supplement; main-text wording per
  tracker Y19 step 4). It never authorizes a replacement generator, φ, grid or seed set (runbook §2.3).

## 11. What is and is not claimed

- **Claimed if MET:** on a second generator, built differently (C × T = 6 × 64, 1/i spectrum, delay-based
  nonseparability), the first-order point registered before any fit lies between the last decided negative
  and the first decided positive contrast at both nonseparable geometries, with a factor-4 grid around it.
- **Not claimed.** The sign of the finite-sample contrast is not *established* by the expansion (Supplement S4.3: "the
  local expansion alone does not establish the sign … on either side of this formal zero"); this is an empirical
  test of the first-order location. No claim about the N\* cell itself, about the exact crossing location
  inside [N\*/2, 2N\*], or about the certificate end of the interval. No claim for estimated covariances
  (oracle Σ only), for non-Gaussian latents (Supplement S4.3: fourth-cumulant terms change c_F), or for other generators.
  No claim of robustness to a mis-specified geometry (see the φ caveat below).
- **Caveats carried into any wording.**
  - *Gaussian/local assumptions.* The constants assume centred Gaussian signal and noise, a positive top gap,
    H ≻ 0 and local convergence of the fitted product branch; the remainders are o(1/N) only in expectation.
  - *Local domain.* A zero at N ≲ 1000 is not justified by the expansion. Both N\* exceed 1000, but the N\*/4
    and N\*/2 cells of B (302, 604) and N\*/4 of A (729) lie below it, and N = 302 is below d = 384 (rank-
    deficient sample covariance; both learners remain defined since N − 1 ≥ k). Those cells test only the sign.
  - *Inherited threshold.* The "N ≲ 1000" limit comes from Supplement S4.3, written for T20-S/T25 (Δ_k = 4,
    d = 256). It is not calibrated for G2, which has half the gap (Δ_k = 2) and d = 384, so G2's limit is
    plausibly higher. N\*_B = 1208 sits at the edge of the inherited limit (the selection rule put it just above
    1200), so B's formal zero itself is at the margin of the local domain. For comparison, T25 θ = 0.25
    (N\* = 1221) is where the Y15 strict grid-adjacency reading failed. The Y19 rule is the fixed-grid analogue
    of Y15's decided-cells reading: it requires the decided sign change inside the factor-2 bracket
    [N\*/2, 2N\*] and leaves the N\* cell unconstrained.
  - *Steep, piecewise-smooth φ dependence.* a(φ) is quadratic near 0 (a = 0.0078, 0.031 at φ = 0.01, 0.02), so
    N\* scales roughly as φ⁻²: N\* moves from 3414 to 2529 between φ = 0.11 and 0.13 (dN\*/dφ ≈ −4.4·10⁴ at A).
    K(φ) is only piecewise smooth, because the pulse-onset kink crosses a sample when φ·δ_c is an integer; the
    first breakpoint is φ = 0.40 (visible in the scan as a 20× drop in da/dφ between 0.40 and 0.41). The
    registered φ = 0.12 and 0.20 lie in the smooth region. The predictions hold only at exactly these φ values,
    and the result says nothing about robustness to a mis-specified geometry.
  - *Best-found optimum.* a_best is a best-found population value (64 restarts agreeing), not a certified
    global optimum; the certificate end of the interval is loose (factor ≈ 3.7 here).
  - *Optimization.* The fitted NFPA is best-found with 4 restarts; its excess includes optimization error that
    c_F does not. Optimization-limited deciding cells are flagged, not excluded.
  - *Construction.* G2 is synthetic; its eigenvectors come from a pulse-template model and its eigenvalues
    are imposed, so φ moves geometry only.
