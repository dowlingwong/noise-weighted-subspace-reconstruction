\begin{abstract}
Label-free scientific reconstruction learns from measured observations alone: each observation supplies its own reconstruction supervision, while the instrument-characterized noise covariance determines which residuals are informative. For Gaussian noise, inverse covariance is the likelihood-consistent metric. Rather than proposing a new estimator, we organize classical methods as a pair $(\Sigma^{-1},\mathcal{F})$ that separates measurement geometry from admissible model capacity, and follow one metric across four classes: optimal filtering, unrestricted linear projection, its Kronecker-separable restriction, and factored attention. The linear classes admit an exact geometric characterization. At the separable class, models sharing an architecture but differing only in the training metric reverse their ranking under raw MSE and the likelihood residual, in a frozen ten-seed experiment and an independent twenty-seed replication, and under a covariance estimated from $2{,}048$ noise-only events. Each model leading under its own objective is the weaker claim; the substantive result is truth-referenced: the covariance-weighted factors recover the \emph{planted} channel subspace almost exactly and decode held-out physical parameters better, while temporal recovery stays weak. A two-seed pilot at the attention class shows no reversal despite non-degenerate weighting; we report that as an open observation, not an architecture-level conclusion.
\end{abstract}
