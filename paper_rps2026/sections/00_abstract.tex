\begin{abstract}
Label-free scientific reconstruction learns representations from measured observations, without class labels or clean targets; instrument-characterized noise covariance supplies the prior for deciding which residuals are informative. For Gaussian noise, inverse covariance is the likelihood-consistent reconstruction metric. Each ingredient is classical --- generalized least squares, the matched filter, weighted PCA, the linear-autoencoder/PCA correspondence, multilinear PCA --- and we introduce no new estimator. We organize them instead as a pair $(\Sigma^{-1},\mathcal{F})$: the likelihood fixes the residual geometry, the architecture the admissible representation class. Holding the metric fixed, we walk $\mathcal{F}$ up four rungs --- optimal filtering, an unrestricted rank-$k$ linear projection (CW-PCA and its tied autoencoder), a Kronecker-separable two-factor projection (NFPA), and factored attention --- and show the metric question recurs at every rung: under non-isotropic noise, raw MSE and the likelihood residual rank equal-capacity models in opposite orders. At the Kronecker rung, two models sharing an architecture and differing only in the training metric reverse across ten seeds, with paired intervals excluding zero in both directions and a twenty-seed white-noise control at exactly zero. The first three rungs admit closed-form analysis; the fourth poses the same question empirically on a public benchmark, where the reversal does not appear: the two objectives fail to separate once whitening is close to a constant rescaling, which locates a condition under which the metric choice stops mattering. A stationary-PSD model that justifies treating $\Sigma$ as diagonal in frequency is adequate on one instrument and fails on another: covariance adequacy is an instrument property, not a framework property.
\end{abstract}
