\subsection{External validity}

All results are conditional on the sampled models, task families, fault
mechanisms, and cost configurations. The controller was trained on R1
development traces and evaluated on R1 confirmatory held-out groups.
\textit{[Discuss whether the controller value transfers to the L4 external
arm and whether the clean-task non-inferiority result holds under
environment shift. Identify any evaluation set where the benefit
disappears.]}

\subsection{Failure cases}

\textit{[Report cases where the adaptive policy made worse decisions than
the fixed baseline: over-verification on low-risk trajectories,
under-verification on rapidly propagating faults, inappropriate recovery

systematic (predictable from the belief state) or stochastic.]}

\subsection{Security and safety implications}

A policy trained to verify and recover could be used to reduce the cost of
operating unreliable agents rather than to improve reliability. The
clean-task regression gate, the verifier-substitution audit, and the
requirement for independent adjudication are structural defenses against
this risk, but they are not guarantees. A deployed version should maintain
the separation between the training reward channel and the operational
safety monitor.

\subsection{Boundary of claims}

H3 and H4 each require specific evidence thresholds (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).
If either threshold is not met, the corresponding claim is narrowed or
withdrawn as specified in the preregistered fallback. Claims are bounded to
the sampled models, tasks, fault regimes, and budget configurations; they
do not extend to substantially different agent architectures, scientific
domains, or cost structures without further evidence.
