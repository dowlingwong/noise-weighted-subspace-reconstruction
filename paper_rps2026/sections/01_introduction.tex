\section{Introduction}

Many scientific instruments produce abundant measurements but no labels or clean targets: each observation is its own training signal, and an instrument-derived noise model says which discrepancies matter. Uncertainty varies across frequencies, sensors and conditions; mean-squared error (MSE) weighs all residual coordinates equally, yet under colored noise a large residual can lie in a poorly measured direction while a small one is decisive. The measurement covariance is therefore a measurement-model prior fixing the geometry of reconstruction error, distinct from a conservation law or equivariance \citep{bronstein2021geometric,karniadakis2021physics}.

This complements self-supervised reconstruction without clean targets \citep{batson2019noise2self,yaman2020ssdu,gruber2025noisier2inverse}, which learn a denoiser rather than vary the residual metric at fixed capacity, and so are context here, not comparators. Augmentation- and masking-based objectives \citep{chen2020simclr,he2022masked} construct supervision by transforming an observation; here the measurement supplies the target and an independently calibrated noise model supplies the residual weighting. We ask a controlled question: if an instrument-derived covariance fixes the training metric, how does its effect depend on the admissible representation class?

Every link is classical, and we state the boundary plainly. Inverse-covariance weighting is generalized least squares \citep{aitken1936least} and, at rank one, the matched filter \citep{turin1960introduction,kay1993fundamentals,szymkowiak1993signal} --- which fixes the weighting but assumes a \emph{known}, one-dimensional template. The latent-variable line relaxes the signal model but not the noise model: probabilistic PCA assumes isotropic noise and factor analysis a diagonal covariance, while weighted low-rank models, Mahalanobis low-rank representation, the linear-autoencoder/PCA correspondence and multilinear PCA are all established \citep{tipping1999probabilistic,rubin1982algorithms,roweis1998algorithms,bailey2012principal,allen2014gls,zhang2019matrixnormal,baldi1989neural,lu2008mpca}. \emph{We introduce no new estimator.} Our contribution is a precise separation of measurement geometry from representation capacity, and the finding it makes testable: at matched capacity the covariance-weighted model's held-out ranking reverses --- reproduced in a disjoint twenty-seed cohort and surviving finite covariance estimation --- and, at the separable condition, it recovers planted structure the isotropic one does not.

The pair $(\Sigma^{-1},\mathcal{F})$ makes that separation precise: the measurement likelihood fixes the residual geometry $\Sigma^{-1}$, the architecture the admissible class $\mathcal{F}$ --- including the axis along which the data are tokenized (Section~3). Holding $\Sigma^{-1}$ fixed, we vary $\mathcal{F}$ across the four classes of Section~3. Among the linear classes the Kronecker-separable family restricts the unrestricted family, and the fixed-template OF case is recovered under Corollary~\ref{cor:rankone}'s assumptions; all three admit an exact geometric characterization (the separable joint optimization is nonconvex; its subproblems are exact), including when the restriction attains the unrestricted optimum (Proposition~\ref{prop:restricted}). The fourth is an analogous axis-factored architecture, examined empirically. Nonlinear classes, distribution shift and adaptive sampling remain future work.
