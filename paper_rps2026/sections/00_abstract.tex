\begin{abstract}
Under additive Gaussian noise and constrained low-rank reconstruction, measured observations can supply their own reconstruction supervision: the instrument-characterized noise covariance then determines which residuals are informative. For Gaussian noise, inverse covariance is the likelihood-consistent metric. Rather than proposing a new estimator, we organize classical methods as a pair $(\Sigma^{-1},\mathcal{F})$ that separates measurement geometry from admissible model capacity, and follow one metric across four classes: optimal filtering, unrestricted linear projection, its Kronecker-separable restriction, and factored attention. The linear classes admit an exact geometric characterization. At the Kronecker-separable class, models sharing an architecture but differing only in the training metric reverse their ranking under raw MSE and the likelihood residual, in a frozen ten-seed experiment and a disjoint twenty-seed replication cohort, and under a covariance estimated from $2{,}048$ noise-only events. Each model leading under its own objective is the weaker claim; the substantive result is truth-referenced: the covariance-weighted factors recover the \emph{planted} channel subspace almost exactly and show better exploratory recovery of planted signal parameters, while temporal recovery stays weak. A two-seed pilot at the attention class shows no reversal despite non-degenerate weighting; we report that as an open observation, not an architecture-level conclusion.
\end{abstract}
