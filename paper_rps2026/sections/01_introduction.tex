\section{Introduction}

Many scientific instruments produce abundant measurements but no labels or clean targets, so each observation is its own training signal while an instrument-derived noise model says which discrepancies are meaningful; the observations are continuous, multi-channel time series whose uncertainty varies across frequencies, sensors, and acquisition conditions. Standard mean-squared error (MSE) weighs all residual coordinates equally; under colored or correlated noise a large residual can lie in a poorly measured direction while a small one in a low-noise direction is statistically decisive. The measurement covariance is therefore a measurement-model prior fixing the geometry of reconstruction error, distinct from a conservation law, equivariance, or simulator constraint \citep{bronstein2021geometric,karniadakis2021physics}.

This complements self-supervised reconstruction without clean targets \citep{batson2019noise2self,yaman2020ssdu,gruber2025noisier2inverse}; unlike augmentation- or masking-based self-supervision \citep{chen2020simclr,he2022masked}, which presupposes nuisance structure that can be simulated, measurement noise supplies that structure directly.

Every individual link here is classical, and we state the boundary plainly. Inverse-covariance weighting is generalized least squares \citep{aitken1936least} and, at rank one, the matched or optimal filter \citep{turin1960introduction,kay1993fundamentals,szymkowiak1993signal}; that literature fixes the weighting but assumes a \emph{known}, one-dimensional template. The latent-variable line relaxes the signal model but not the noise model: probabilistic PCA assumes isotropic noise \citep{tipping1999probabilistic} and factor analysis a diagonal covariance \citep{rubin1982algorithms,roweis1998algorithms}, while weighted low-rank models \citep{bailey2012principal,allen2014gls} and the linear-autoencoder/PCA correspondence \citep{baldi1989neural} are established, as is multilinear PCA \citep{lu2008mpca}. \emph{We introduce no new estimator.} What is new is the arrangement: placing these in one $\Sigma^{-1}$ metric as a ladder of admissible representation classes, the exact condition under which the Kronecker restriction is lossless (Proposition~\ref{prop:restricted}), and the observation that a factored-attention network is that same restriction relaxed to a nonlinear map --- so the metric question does not stop where closed-form analysis does.

We ask: \emph{what geometry should a label-free scientific reconstruction use under structured noise, and how should that geometry interact with the architecture that expresses the representation?} We organize reconstruction methods as a pair $(\Sigma^{-1},\mathcal{F})$: the measurement likelihood fixes the residual geometry $\Sigma^{-1}$, while the architecture fixes the admissible representation class $\mathcal{F}$ --- including, for structured measurements, the axis along which the data are tokenized (Section~3). Holding $\Sigma^{-1}$ fixed, we walk $\mathcal{F}$ up the four rungs of Section~3: optimal filtering, unrestricted rank-$k$ linear (Proposition~1), Kronecker-separable two-factor (Proposition~2), and factored attention. The first three rungs admit closed-form analysis; the fourth is settled empirically, and under non-isotropic noise raw MSE and the likelihood residual can rank equal-capacity models in opposite orders (Section~2). Nonlinear representation classes beyond attention, distribution shift, and adaptive sampling remain future work.
