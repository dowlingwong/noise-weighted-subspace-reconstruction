\begin{abstract}
Label-free scientific reconstruction learns representations from measured observations, without class labels or clean targets, and instrument-characterized noise covariance supplies the prior for deciding which residuals are informative. For Gaussian noise, inverse covariance is the likelihood-consistent reconstruction metric. Every ingredient here is classical --- generalized least squares, the matched filter, weighted PCA, the linear-autoencoder/PCA correspondence, multilinear PCA --- and we introduce no new estimator; we organize them as a pair $(\Sigma^{-1},\mathcal{F})$, the likelihood fixing the residual geometry and the architecture the admissible representation class. Holding the metric fixed, we walk $\mathcal{F}$ up four rungs: optimal filtering, an unrestricted rank-$k$ linear projection, a Kronecker-separable two-factor projection, and factored attention. At the linear rungs the metric question has a closed-form answer and reverses in practice: two models sharing an architecture and differing only in the training metric rank oppositely under raw MSE and the likelihood residual, across ten seeds with paired intervals excluding zero and a twenty-seed white-noise control at exactly zero. At the attention rung the reversal does not appear, and the obvious explanation --- a near-degenerate whitening there --- is ruled out by measurement. The metric fixes the geometry; whether that choice bites is a property of the instrument and the representation.
\end{abstract}
