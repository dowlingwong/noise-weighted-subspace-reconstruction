\paragraph{The controlled experiment.}
Each event is a $C=8$ channel $\times$ $T=256$ sample trace: a pulse with per-event amplitude, rise and decay time, generated around a planted rank-$(k_c,k_t)=(2,3)$ factorization and corrupted by Brownian $1/f^2$ noise with correlated channels at SNR $10$; $800/400/2000$ events train, validate and test; each seed resamples signals, noise and splits. \emph{Two conditions, not to be conflated}: the reversal experiment uses nonseparability $\alpha=0.1$; truth-referenced recovery is evaluated separately at the exactly separable $\alpha=0$. $\Sigma$ is oracle unless stated, and every interval resamples \emph{seeds} at $95\%$ (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}).

\paragraph{NFPA under channel compression.}
We compare NFPA, an isotropically trained factored baseline of the same architecture, CW-PCA and PCA at matched rank $6$. NFPA and the isotropic baseline differ only in the training metric, and it reverses: in the frozen ten-seed experiment NFPA is better under the likelihood residual ($-0.0192$, 95\% interval $[-0.0194,-0.0190]$) and worse under raw MSE ($+0.0034$, $[+0.0034,+0.0034]$), with narrow seed-bootstrap intervals excluding zero (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}). A disjoint twenty-seed cohort, same pipeline and configuration, agrees in sign and magnitude ($-0.0190$, $[-0.0192,-0.0189]$; $+0.0034$); cohorts are reported separately; a pooled figure would be post-hoc. A twenty-seed white-noise control returns exactly zero, as it must at $\Sigma=\sigma^2I$. Estimating the covariance from $2{,}048$ noise-only events preserves the reversal in all ten seeds, increasing NFPA's likelihood residual and raw MSE by $0.0078\%$ and $0.0345\%$ respectively of its own oracle values --- one fixed condition, not a sweep (Table~\ref{tab:round10}).

\paragraph{Rung 4 (two-seed pilot): the metric in a factored-attention network.}
Two seeds train one factored-attention architecture on the TIDMAD readout under objectives differing only in the metric. \emph{The reversal does not appear at either seed}: the $\Sigma^{-1}$-trained arm is better on \emph{both} criteria ($-1.15$ and $-1.17$, both $\times10^{-4}$, on the likelihood residual; $-57.2$ and $-57.4$ on raw MSE, the latter opposite to prediction). The coarse score cannot separate the arms either (Table~\ref{tab:rung4}). Of three explanations --- degenerate weighting, the architecture, or the instrument's band restriction --- only the first is testable here, and measurement rules it out: the per-frequency inverse-noise weights have coefficient of variation $0.69$ (range factor $441$), the same order as the rung-3 geometry where the reversal \emph{is} observed ($1.10$; white control $0$). Two seeds cannot separate the other two, and both arms sit far below published baselines (Appendix~\ref{app:rung4-baselines}): this bounds the claim, and is not evidence that architecture decides whether the metric matters.

\paragraph{What the covariance-weighted factors recover.}
The metric also changes what the representation \emph{is}, and these quantities are truth-referenced: unlike the ranking above, none of them follows from each model leading under its own objective. At the exactly separable condition ($\alpha=0$) NFPA recovers the planted channel subspace almost exactly ($0.035$ projector error against the isotropic $1.021$) while temporal recovery stays weak ($0.768$ against $1.280$) --- an asymmetry a joint number would hide. All seven paired contrasts favour NFPA, every across-seed bootstrap interval excludes zero and all ten seeds agree in sign, with no multiplicity correction; two are predeclared (the channel and temporal projector errors) and five post-hoc. No recovery threshold was predeclared either way, so these are continuous estimates, not a verdict (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}).

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
Under additive Gaussian noise inverse covariance is the likelihood-consistent geometry for constrained linear reconstruction. In the controlled setting it changes both the learned subspace and the recovery of planted structure. Separately, the metric reversal persists under one finite-covariance estimate. Whether it survives into competitive nonlinear reconstruction is open --- our one pilot did not reproduce it --- as is transfer: the same machinery, calibrated synthetically, is over-dispersed on real gravitational-wave strain \citep{gwoscgw150914,macleod2021gwpy}, so implementation identity is not calibration (Appendix~\ref{app:transfer}).
